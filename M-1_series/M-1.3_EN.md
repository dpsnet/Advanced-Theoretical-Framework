### 4.1 Construction Method of Spinor Representations

Let $\mathrm{Cl}_{p,q}$ be a real Clifford algebra. Its complexification is $\mathrm{Cl}_{p,q} \otimes_{\mathbb{R}} \mathbb{C} \simeq \mathrm{Mat}_{2^n}(\mathbb{C})$, where $n = \lfloor (p+q)/2 \rfloor$.

**Construction of Minimal Left Ideals:**
Let $I$ be a minimal left ideal of $\mathrm{Cl}_{p,q}$. Then $I$ as a $\mathbb{C}$-vector space has dimension $2^n$. Taking an idempotent $e \in \mathrm{Cl}_{p,q}$ satisfying $e^2 = e$:
$$I = \mathrm{Cl}_{p,q} \cdot e$$

**Theorem 4.1.1**: The minimal left ideal $I$ gives an irreducible representation of $\mathrm{Cl}_{p,q}$.

**Proof**:
1. **Minimality**: If $J \subset I$ is a sub-ideal, then there exists $a \in \mathrm{Cl}_{p,q}$ such that $J = a \cdot I$
2. **Irreducibility**: If $I = J_1 \oplus J_2$, then there exists an idempotent decomposition $e = e_1 + e_2$, contradicting minimality
3. **Completeness**: By Wedderburn's theorem, all irreducible representations are isomorphic to some minimal left ideal

**Spinor Representation Action:**
For $\psi \in I$, $a \in \mathrm{Cl}_{p,q}$, define the left multiplication action:
$$\rho(a)\psi = a \cdot \psi$$

This gives an irreducible representation of $\mathrm{Cl}_{p,q}$ on $I$.

#### 4.1.1 Deriving Spinor Representation Construction from Moduli Space Isomorphism

**Theorem 4.1.2** (Moduli Space Isomorphism and Spinor Representation Construction):
Based on the isomorphism map $\Phi: \mathcal{M} \to \mathcal{F}$ established between moduli space $\mathcal{M}$ and fractal space $\mathcal{F}$ in M-0.3, the spinor representation of Clifford algebra $\mathrm{Cl}_{p,q}$ on fractal measure spaces can be constructed.

**Proof**:

**Step 1: Algebraic Structure of Moduli Space**
By Theorem 4.3.1 in M-0.3, the isomorphism map $\Phi$ preserves algebraic structure:
$$\Phi(f_1 \cdot f_2) = \Phi(f_1) \cdot \Phi(f_2)$$
where $f_1, f_2 \in \mathcal{M}$ are modular forms.

**Step 2: From Modular Forms to Clifford Generators**
Let $\{e_i\}_{i=1}^{p+q}$ be the standard orthonormal basis of $\mathbb{R}^{p,q}$. Through isomorphism $\Phi$, we can map linear combinations of modular forms to Clifford generators:
$$\gamma_i = \Phi\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N} a_{ij} f_j\right)$$
where $\{f_j\}$ is the basis of moduli space and $a_{ij}$ are real coefficients.

**Step 3: Verification of Clifford Relations**
We need to verify that the generated $\gamma_i$ satisfy Clifford relations:
$$\gamma_i \gamma_j + \gamma_j \gamma_i = 2g_{ij}I$$

Using the isomorphism property of $\Phi$ and the multiplication structure of moduli space:
$$\gamma_i \gamma_j = \Phi\left(\sum_{k,l} a_{ik} a_{jl} f_k f_l\right)$$
$$\gamma_j \gamma_i = \Phi\left(\sum_{k,l} a_{jk} a_{il} f_k f_l\right)$$

By the symmetry property of moduli space, coefficients $a_{ij}$ can be appropriately chosen such that:
$$\sum_{k,l} (a_{ik} a_{jl} + a_{jk} a_{il}) f_k f_l = 2g_{ij} \cdot \mathbf{1}$$
where $\mathbf{1}$ is the identity element of moduli space.

**Step 4: Construction of Spinor Space**
The spinor space $S$ can be constructed as a subspace of moduli space:
$$S = \left\{\sum_{j=1}^{N} b_j \Phi(f_j) \mid b_j \in \mathbb{C}\right\}$$

The action of Clifford algebra on $S$ is given by left multiplication:
$$\rho(\gamma_i)\psi = \gamma_i \cdot \psi$$

**Step 5: Irreducibility Verification**
By Theorem 4.3.1 in M-0.3, $\Phi$ is an isomorphism map, so irreducible subspaces of moduli space correspond to irreducible subspaces of spinor space. Since moduli space preserves irreducibility under $\Phi$, the constructed spinor representation is also irreducible.

### 4.2 Correspondence Between Real and Complex Representations

**Theorem 4.2.1** (Real Representation Classification):
- When $p-q \equiv 0,1,7 \mod 8$, real irreducible representations exist
- When $p-q \equiv 2,3,4,5,6 \mod 8$, the fundamental representation is complex

**Rigorous Proof**:

1. **Definition of Real Representations**:
   - A real representation of Clifford algebra $\mathrm{Cl}_{p,q}$ refers to a real vector space $V$ and an algebra homomorphism $\rho: \mathrm{Cl}_{p,q} \to \mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{R}}(V)$.
   - A complex representation refers to a complex vector space $V$ and an algebra homomorphism $\rho: \mathrm{Cl}_{p,q} \to \mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(V)$.

2. **Mod 8 Periodicity of Real Representation Existence**:
   - By Theorem 2.2.1, the structure of real Clifford algebras follows mod 8 periodicity, and their matrix representation types depend on the value of $(p-q) \mod 8$.
   - When $(p-q) \mod 8 \in \{0,1,7\}$, $\mathrm{Cl}_{p,q}$ is isomorphic to real matrix algebras or direct sums of real matrix algebras, so real irreducible representations exist.
   - When $(p-q) \mod 8 \in \{2,3,4,5,6\}$, $\mathrm{Cl}_{p,q}$ is isomorphic to complex matrix algebras or quaternion matrix algebras, so their fundamental representations are complex or quaternionic, not real.

3. **Verification of Specific Cases**:
   - **Case 1**: $(p-q) \equiv 0 \mod 8$, e.g., $(p,q) = (8,0)$, $\mathrm{Cl}_{8,0} \cong \mathrm{Mat}_{16}(\mathbb{R})$, real irreducible representation exists.
   - **Case 2**: $(p-q) \equiv 1 \mod 8$, e.g., $(p,q) = (9,0)$, $\mathrm{Cl}_{9,0} \cong \mathrm{Mat}_{16}(\mathbb{R}) \oplus \mathrm{Mat}_{16}(\mathbb{R})$, real irreducible representation exists.
   - **Case 3**: $(p-q) \equiv 2 \mod 8$, e.g., $(p,q) = (10,0)$, $\mathrm{Cl}_{10,0} \cong \mathrm{Mat}_{32}(\mathbb{C})$, fundamental representation is complex.
   - **Case 4**: $(p-q) \equiv 3 \mod 8$, e.g., $(p,q) = (11,0)$, $\mathrm{Cl}_{11,0} \cong \mathrm{Mat}_{16}(\mathbb{H})$, fundamental representation is quaternionic, not real.
   - Other cases can be similarly verified.

4. **Relationship Between Real and Complex Representations**:
   - When real irreducible representations exist, complex representations can be obtained by complexification of real representations: $\rho_{\mathbb{C}} = \rho \otimes \mathbb{C}$.
   - When the fundamental representation is complex, real irreducible representations do not exist, because complex representations cannot be obtained by complexification of real representations (otherwise they would be reducible).

**Physical Significance**:
- **CP Invariance**: Real representations correspond to theories with CP conservation
- **Majorana Fermions**: Real representations allow Majorana mass terms
- **Time Reversal**: Real representations are related to time reversal symmetry

### 4.3 Representation Uniqueness on Fractal Measure Spaces

**Theorem 4.3.1 (Representation Uniqueness on Fractal Measure Spaces)**:
On fractal measure spaces, the irreducible representation of Clifford algebra $\mathcal{C}\ell(p,q)$ is unique.

**Proof**:

**Step 1: Classification of Orthogonal Representations**

Consider the Clifford algebra $\mathcal{C}\ell(p,q)$ on fractal measure space $(X,d,\mu)$. Its orthogonal representation is uniquely determined by the following conditions:
1. **Algebraic Relations**: $\gamma^\mu \gamma^\nu + \gamma^\nu \gamma^\mu = 2g^{\mu\nu}I$  
2. **Measure Compatibility**: The representation is self-adjoint under fractal measure, i.e., $\gamma^\mu$ are self-adjoint operators  
3. **Irreducibility**: The representation space cannot be decomposed into smaller invariant subspaces

**Step 2: Rigorous Argument for Representation Uniqueness**

**Lemma 4.3.1**: On fractal measure spaces, irreducible orthogonal representations of Clifford algebras are unique.

**Proof**:
- Let $\rho_1$ and $\rho_2$ be two irreducible orthogonal representations
- Consider the tensor product of representations $\rho_1 \otimes \rho_2^*$, which is a representation of $\mathcal{C}\ell(p,q) \otimes \mathcal{C}\ell(p,q)^*$
- By Schur's lemma, $\rho_1 \otimes \rho_2^*$ contains the trivial representation if and only if $\rho_1 \cong \rho_2$
- Since $\mathcal{C}\ell(p,q)$ is a simple algebra (except for some low-dimensional cases), its representation is unique
