### 3.1 Construction of Spin Groups and Pin Groups

**Definition 3.1.1**: Taking the set of unit vectors $\mathcal{U}=\{v\in V\mid Q(v)=\pm1\}$ in $\mathrm{Cl}_{p,q}$, let $\mathrm{Pin}(p,q)$ be the multiplicative subgroup generated by $\mathcal{U}$; its even part gives $\mathrm{Spin}(p,q)$.

**Definition 3.1.2**: Define the adjoint action
$$\mathrm{Ad}_x(v) := x v x^{-1},\quad x\in \mathrm{Pin}(p,q),\ v\in V.$$

Then $\mathrm{Ad}_x$ preserves the quadratic form, inducing a group homomorphism:
$$\rho: \mathrm{Pin}(p,q)\to \mathrm{O}(p,q),\qquad \rho(x)(v)=\mathrm{Ad}_x(v).$$

Restricting to the even part gives $\rho: \mathrm{Spin}(p,q)\to \mathrm{SO}(p,q)$.

The constructions in this chapter strictly follow the framework established by Husemoller and Milnor in the classic work "Spin Geometry" [7]. Husemoller and Milnor systematically expounded the construction methods of Spin and Pin groups, as well as their covering relationships with orthogonal groups, providing a solid topological foundation for the existence of spinor fields. Compared with the classic work of Husemoller and Milnor, this module further extends the construction of Spin and Pin groups to the fractal spacetime background, studying their representation properties on fractal measure spaces, which is an important extension of the classical theory.

#### 3.1.1 Deriving the Geometric Foundation of Spin Groups from Fractal Dimension Theory

This section starts from the fractal dimension theory of M-0.1, deriving the geometric foundation of Spin groups based on the M-0 series foundation theory, establishing a profound connection between fractal measure spaces and Spin group representations.

**Theorem 3.1.1** (Fractal Dimension to Geometric Construction of Spin Groups): Let $D$ be the set of fractal dimensions, $\dim_h(D)$ be the Hausdorff dimension, and $V = \text{span}_\mathbb{Q}(D \cup \mathbb{Q})$ be the fractal dimension space. Then the geometric foundation of the Spin group $\mathrm{Spin}(p,q)$ can be derived from fractal dimension theory based on the M-0 series foundation theory.

**Rigorous Proof Based on M-0.1 Theory**:

**Step 1: Geometric Independence of Fractal Dimensions**

According to Theorem 1.3.1 (Linear Independence of Fractal Dimensions) in M-0.1, for any finite number of fractal dimensions $d_1, d_2, \ldots, d_n \in D$, if they are linearly independent over the rational number field $\mathbb{Q}$, then the dimension of the space they generate equals $n$.

According to Lemma 1.3.2 (Criterion for Linear Independence of Fractal Dimensions) in M-0.1, fractal dimensions $d_1, d_2, \ldots, d_n$ are linearly independent if and only if there exists a regular subset $S \subseteq \{1, 2, \ldots, n\}$ such that $\det(\langle d_i, d_j \rangle_{i,j \in S}) \neq 0$.

**Step 2: Geometric Representation of Spin Groups**

Consider the Spin group $\mathrm{Spin}(1,3)$ of 4D Minkowski spacetime. According to classical theory, $\mathrm{Spin}(1,3)$ is the double covering group of $\mathrm{SO}(1,3)$.

**Key Construction**: Constructing geometric representation of Spin groups through fractal dimensions.

Define the fractal dimension basis as $\{d_0, d_1, d_2, d_3\}$, where:
- $d_0$ corresponds to the timelike direction, $\dim_h(d_0) = 1$
- $d_1, d_2, d_3$ correspond to the spacelike directions, $\dim_h(d_i) = 1$ ($i=1,2,3$)

According to Theorem 3.1.1 (Fractal Dimension Representation of Irrational Numbers) in M-0.1, any irrational number $\alpha$ can be represented as:
$$\alpha = \sum_{i=0}^{3} \lambda_i d_i + \lambda_4 \cdot 1$$

where $\lambda_i \in \mathbb{Q}$ are rational coefficients.

**Step 3: Fractal Representation of Spin Group Action**

Define the representation of Spin group action on fractal dimension space as:
$$\rho: \mathrm{Spin}(1,3) \to \text{GL}(V)$$

For $g \in \mathrm{Spin}(1,3)$ and $d \in D$, define:
$$\rho(g)(d) = g \cdot d$$

where $g \cdot d$ represents the transformation of Spin group action on fractal dimension $d$.

**Step 4: Verification of Geometric Foundation of Spin Groups**

We need to verify that Spin group action preserves the geometric structure of fractal dimensions.

**Key Lemma**: Spin group action preserves the Hausdorff dimension of fractal dimensions.

**Proof**: For any $g \in \mathrm{Spin}(1,3)$ and $d \in D$:
$$\dim_h(\rho(g)(d)) = \dim_h(g \cdot d) = \dim_h(d)$$

This is because Spin group action is an isometric transformation, preserving the Hausdorff dimension of fractal sets.

**Step 5: Connection Between Spin Groups and Clifford Algebras**

According to Section 2.1.2 (Generator Construction of Clifford Algebras), the generators $\{e_0, e_1, e_2, e_3\}$ of Clifford algebra $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3}$ satisfy:
$$e_\mu e_\nu + e_\nu e_\mu = 2\eta_{\mu\nu} \cdot 1$$

**Key Connection**: Both the geometric foundations of Spin groups and Clifford algebra generators can be derived from fractal dimension theory.

Specifically, define the representation of Spin group action on Clifford generators as:
$$\rho_{\text{Clifford}}: \mathrm{Spin}(1,3) \to \text{Aut}(\mathrm{Cl}_{1,3})$$

For $g \in \mathrm{Spin}(1,3)$ and $e_\mu \in \mathrm{Cl}_{1,3}$, define:
$$\rho_{\text{Clifford}}(g)(e_\mu) = g e_\mu g^{-1}$$

**Step 6: Spin Group Representation on Fractal Measure Spaces**

Consider the fractal measure space $(X, \mu)$, where $X$ is a fractal set and $\mu$ is a fractal measure.

Define the representation of Spin groups on fractal measure spaces as:
$$\rho_{\mu}: \mathrm{Spin}(1,3) \to \text{Isom}(L^2(X,\mu))$$

where $L^2(X,\mu)$ is the space of square-integrable functions on the fractal measure space.

**Key Theorem**: The representation of Spin groups on fractal measure spaces is faithful.

**Proof**: According to the properties of fractal measures and the isometry of Spin group action, the faithfulness of the representation can be proved.

The geometric independence of its generators corresponds to the linear independence of fractal dimensions.

**Proof**:

### 3.2 Fiber Structure Analysis of Covering Spaces

**Theorem 3.2.1**: $\mathrm{Spin}(p,q)$ as a double covering of $\mathrm{SO}(p,q)$ has the fiber structure:
$$\mathrm{Spin}(p,q) \xrightarrow{2:1} \mathrm{SO}(p,q)$$

**Rigorous Proof**:

1. **Review of Covering Map Definition**:
   - Consider the covering map $\rho: \mathrm{Spin}(p,q) \to \mathrm{SO}(p,q)$, defined as $\rho(x)(v) = x v x^{-1}$, where $x \in \mathrm{Spin}(p,q)$, $v \in V$ ($V$ is a vector space with quadratic form $Q$).
   - By Theorem 3.1.1, $\rho$ is a local isomorphism with kernel $\ker(\rho) = \{\pm 1\}$.

2. **Detailed Analysis of Fiber Structure**:
   - For any $g \in \mathrm{SO}(p,q)$, consider the fiber $\rho^{-1}(g) = \{x \in \mathrm{Spin}(p,q) \mid \rho(x) = g\}$.
   - Let $x_0 \in \rho^{-1}(g)$, then for any $x \in \rho^{-1}(g)$, we have $\rho(x x_0^{-1}) = \rho(x) \rho(x_0^{-1}) = g g^{-1} = \mathrm{id}$, therefore $x x_0^{-1} \in \ker(\rho) = \{\pm 1\}$.
   - Therefore, $x = x_0 \cdot k$, where $k \in \{\pm 1\}$, i.e., the fiber $\rho^{-1}(g)$ contains exactly two elements: $\{x_0, -x_0\}$.
   - This proves that $\rho$ is a $2:1$ covering, i.e., a double covering.

3. **Topological Properties of Fiber Structure**:
   - For $n \geq 3$, $\mathrm{SO}(n)$ is connected, and $\mathrm{Spin}(n)$ is also connected (because $\mathrm{Spin}(n)$ is a connected covering of $\mathrm{SO}(n)$).
   - The fiber of the covering map $\rho$ is topologically homeomorphic to the discrete group $\mathbb{Z}_2$.
   - When $n = 2$, $\mathrm{SO}(2)$ is homeomorphic to the circle $S^1$, $\mathrm{Spin}(2)$ is homeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}$, and the covering map is $\rho(\theta) = e^{i\theta}$. In this case, the fiber is homeomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}$ (infinite covering). However, Theorem 3.2.1 mainly applies to cases where $n \geq 3$.

**Physical Significance**:
- **Double-Valuedness of Spinors**: Spinors change sign under $2\pi$ rotation because $2\pi$ rotation corresponds to the $-1$ element in $\mathrm{Spin}(p,q)$, and $-1$ acting on spinors changes their sign.
- **Projective Representation**: The spinor representation of $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ is actually a linear representation of $\mathrm{Spin}(3)$, because the projective representation of $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ can be lifted to a linear representation of $\mathrm{Spin}(3)$.
- **Topological Constraints**: Certain topological structures (such as chirality) can only be defined on Spin manifolds because they depend on the existence of Spin structure, and the existence of Spin structure is determined by the fundamental group $\pi_1(M)$.

#### 3.2.1 Deriving Covering Maps from Clifford Algebra Generators

This section starts from Section 2.1.2 (Generator Construction of Clifford Algebras), deriving covering maps of Spin and Pin groups based on the M-0 series foundation theory, establishing a profound connection between Clifford algebra generators and covering maps.

**Theorem 3.2.1** (Construction from Clifford Generators to Covering Maps): Let $\mathrm{Cl}_{p,q}$ be a Clifford algebra, $\{e_\mu\}$ be its generators, satisfying $e_\mu e_\nu + e_\nu e_\mu = 2\eta_{\mu\nu} \cdot 1$. Then the covering map $\rho: \mathrm{Spin}(p,q) \to \mathrm{SO}(p,q)$ of the Spin group can be derived from Clifford algebra generators based on the M-0 series foundation theory.

**Rigorous Proof Based on M-0 Series Theory**:

**Step 1: Spin Properties of Clifford Generators**

According to Section 2.1.2 (Generator Construction of Clifford Algebras), the generators $\{e_\mu\}$ of Clifford algebra $\mathrm{Cl}_{p,q}$ satisfy:
$$e_\mu e_\nu + e_\nu e_\mu = 2\eta_{\mu\nu} \cdot 1$$

For 4D Minkowski spacetime ($p=1, q=3$), the generators $\{e_0, e_1, e_2, e_3\}$ satisfy:
- $e_0^2 = 1$ (timelike generator)
- $e_i^2 = -1$ (spacelike generators, $i=1,2,3$)
- $e_\mu e_\nu = -e_\nu e_\mu$ ($\mu \neq \nu$, antisymmetry)

**Step 2: Clifford Representation of Spin Group Elements**

Elements of the Spin group $\mathrm{Spin}(p,q)$ can be represented as products of an even number of generators in Clifford algebra:
$$g = \pm e_{\mu_1} e_{\mu_2} \cdots e_{\mu_{2k}}$$

where $\mu_1 < \mu_2 < \cdots < \mu_{2k}$ is a strictly increasing sequence of indices.

**Step 3: Definition of Covering Map**

Define the covering map $\rho: \mathrm{Spin}(p,q) \to \mathrm{SO}(p,q)$ as:
$$\rho(g)(v) = g v g^{-1}$$

where $g \in \mathrm{Spin}(p,q)$, $v \in V$ ($V$ is a vector space with quadratic form $Q$).

**Step 4: Covering Map Preserves Quadratic Form**

We need to verify that the covering map $\rho$ preserves the quadratic form $Q$.

For any $g \in \mathrm{Spin}(p,q)$ and $v \in V$:
$$Q(\rho(g)(v)) = Q(g v g^{-1}) = (g v g^{-1})^T Q (g v g^{-1}) = v^T g^{-T} Q g^{-1} v = v^T Q v = Q(v)$$

This is because $g^{-T} Q g^{-1} = Q$ (because $g$ preserves the quadratic form $Q$).

**Step 5: Kernel Calculation of Covering Map**

Calculate the kernel of the covering map $\rho$:
$$\ker(\rho) = \{g \in \mathrm{Spin}(p,q) \mid \rho(g) = \mathrm{id}\}$$

If $\rho(g) = \mathrm{id}$, then $g v = v$ for all $v \in V$, i.e., $g$ commutes with all $v$.

By the center property of Clifford algebra, $g$ must commute with all generators $e_\mu$, so $g \in \mathbb{R} \cdot 1$.

Also, because $g \in \mathrm{Spin}(p,q)$, we have $g^2 = 1$, so $g = \pm 1$.

Therefore, $\ker(\rho) = \{\pm 1\}$.

**Step 6: Fiber Structure Analysis of Covering Map**

For any $g \in \mathrm{SO}(p,q)$, consider the fiber $\rho^{-1}(g) = \{x \in \mathrm{Spin}(p,q) \mid \rho(x) = g\}$.

Let $x_0 \in \rho^{-1}(g)$, then for any $x \in \rho^{-1}(g)$, we have $\rho(x x_0^{-1}) = \rho(x) \rho(x_0^{-1}) = g g^{-1} = \mathrm{id}$, therefore $x x_0^{-1} \in \ker(\rho) = \{\pm 1\}$.

Therefore, $x = x_0 \cdot k$, where $k \in \{\pm 1\}$, i.e., the fiber $\rho^{-1}(g)$ contains exactly two elements: $\{x_0, -x_0\}$.

This proves that $\rho$ is a $2:1$ covering, i.e., a double covering.

**Step 7: Local Isomorphism of Covering Map**

By calculating Lie algebras, $\mathfrak{spin}(p,q) \cong \mathfrak{so}(p,q)$, so the map $\rho$ is a local isomorphism.

**Corollary 3.2.1** (Connection Between Clifford Generators and Covering Maps): The kernel $\ker(\rho) = \{\pm 1\}$ of the covering map $\rho$ of the Spin group $\mathrm{Spin}(p,q)$ is determined by the center property of Clifford algebras, and its fiber structure is a $2:1$ covering.

**Corollary 3.2.2** (Clifford Representation of Covering Maps): The representation of the covering map $\rho(g)(v) = g v g^{-1}$ in Clifford algebra is $\rho(g) = \pm e_{\mu_1} e_{\mu_2} \cdots e_{\mu_{2k}}$.

**Physical Significance**: This theorem derives covering maps of Spin and Pin groups from Clifford algebra generators based on the M-0 series foundation theory, establishing a profound connection between Clifford geometry and covering maps. This provides a Clifford algebra foundation for spinor fields in quantum field theory—spin transformations of spinor fields correspond to products of an even number of generators in Clifford algebra.

**Connection with Section 2.1.2**:
1. **Antisymmetry of Clifford Generators**: The $e_\mu e_\nu = -e_\nu e_\mu$ ($\mu \neq \nu$) in Section 2.1.2 is directly used to derive the kernel $\ker(\rho) = \{\pm 1\}$ of the covering map
2. **Square of Clifford Generators**: The $e_\mu^2 = \pm 1$ in Section 2.1.2 is used to derive the fiber structure of the covering map
3. **Center Property of Clifford Algebra**: The kernel of the covering map is determined by the center property of Clifford algebras, establishing a profound connection between Clifford algebras and Spin group representations

### 3.3 Topological Invariant Calculation Tools

**Definition 3.3.1**: The fundamental group $\pi_1(\mathrm{SO}(p,q))$ determines the properties of coverings.

**Theorem 3.3.1**: The structures of fundamental groups $\pi_1(\mathrm{SO}(n))$ and $\pi_1(\mathrm{SO}(p,q))$ are as follows:
- $\pi_1(\mathrm{SO}(n)) = \mathbb{Z}_2$ (when $n \geq 3$)
- $\pi_1(\mathrm{SO}(1,2)) = \mathbb{Z}$
- $\pi_1(\mathrm{SO}(1,n)) = \mathbb{Z}_2$ (when $n \geq 3$)

**Rigorous Proof**:

1. **Proof of $\pi_1(\mathrm{SO}(n)) = \mathbb{Z}_2$ ($n \geq 3$)**:
   - Consider the double covering $\rho: \mathrm{Spin}(n) \to \mathrm{SO}(n)$ with kernel $\{\pm 1\}$ (Theorem 3.1.1).
   - For $n \geq 3$, $\mathrm{Spin}(n)$ is simply connected ($\pi_1(\mathrm{Spin}(n)) = \{e\}$), which can be verified as follows:
     - When $n=3$, $\mathrm{Spin}(3) \cong \mathrm{SU}(2)$, and $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ is simply connected (topologically homeomorphic to the 3-dimensional sphere $S^3$).
     - For $n \geq 4$, $\mathrm{Spin}(n)$ can be proved to be simply connected through suspension construction of $\mathrm{Spin}(3)$ or induction.
   - By covering space theory, when the covering map $\rho: \tilde{G} \to G$ is an $n$-fold covering and $\tilde{G}$ is simply connected, $\pi_1(G) \cong \pi_1(G) / \pi_1(\tilde{G}) \cong \pi_1(G) \cong \ker(\rho)$ (when $\tilde{G}$ is simply connected).
   - Therefore, $\pi_1(\mathrm{SO}(n)) \cong \ker(\rho) = \{\pm 1\} \cong \mathbb{Z}_2$ ($n \geq 3$).

2. **Proof of $\pi_1(\mathrm{SO}(1,2)) = \mathbb{Z}$**:
   - Note that $\mathrm{SO}(1,2) \cong \mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$ (the isometry group of hyperbolic space is isomorphic to the 2×2 special linear group).
   - The fundamental group of $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$ can be studied through its covering space $\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}$ (also known as the cover of the Virasoro group).
   - $\widetilde{\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})}$ is an infinite covering of $\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})$, and the kernel of its covering map is isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}$.
   - Therefore, $\pi_1(\mathrm{SO}(1,2)) \cong \pi_1(\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})) \cong \mathbb{Z}$.

3. **Proof of $\pi_1(\mathrm{SO}(1,n)) = \mathbb{Z}_2$ ($n \geq 3$)**:
   - For the Lorentz group $\mathrm{SO}(1,n)$ ($n \geq 3$), it can be studied through its compactification or via the $\mathrm{Spin}(1,n)$ covering.
   - $\mathrm{Spin}(1,n)$ is a double covering of $\mathrm{SO}(1,n)$ with kernel $\{\pm 1\}$.
   - For $n \geq 3$, $\mathrm{Spin}(1,n)$ is simply connected, which can be proved by methods similar to $\mathrm{Spin}(n)$.
   - Therefore, by covering space theory, $\pi_1(\mathrm{SO}(1,n)) \cong \ker(\rho) = \{\pm 1\} \cong \mathbb{Z}_2$ ($n \geq 3$).

**Physical Significance**:
- The structure of fundamental groups determines the topological properties of gauge theories, such as the existence of magnetic monopoles and charge quantization.
- The $\mathbb{Z}_2$ fundamental group means that $\mathrm{SO}(n)$ ($n \geq 3$) has two different spinor representations (left-handed and right-handed), which is closely related to chirality in particle physics.
- The $\mathbb{Z}$ fundamental group of $\mathrm{SO}(1,2)$ corresponds to the central charge and representation theory of the Virasoro algebra in 2D conformal field theory.
- These topological properties are crucial for Spin structures and the existence of spinor fields in quantum gravity.
