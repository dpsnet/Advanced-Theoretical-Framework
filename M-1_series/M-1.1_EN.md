### 2.1 Definition and Construction of Real Clifford Algebras

Let $(V,Q)$ be a finite-dimensional real vector space with its quadratic form. The real Clifford algebra is defined as the quotient space:
$$\mathrm{Cl}(V,Q)=T(V)/\langle v\otimes v- Q(v)\cdot 1\rangle$$

where $T(V) = \bigoplus_{k=0}^{\infty} V^{\otimes k}$ is the tensor algebra, and the ideal is generated by elements $v\otimes v - Q(v)\cdot 1$. Clifford multiplication satisfies the fundamental relation:
$$uv+vu=2\langle u,v\rangle, \quad \forall u,v \in V$$

Let $p,q$ be the signatures (number of positive and negative eigenvalues) of $Q$, and denote $\mathrm{Cl}_{p,q} = \mathrm{Cl}(\mathbb{R}^{p,q},Q)$.

The definitions and constructions in this chapter strictly follow the framework established by Chevalley in the classic work "The Algebraic Theory of Spinors and Clifford Algebras" [1]. Chevalley systematically expounded the algebraic theory of Clifford algebras, laying a solid foundation for subsequent constructions of spinor representations. Compared with Chevalley's classic work, this module further extends Clifford algebras to the fractal spacetime background, studying their representation properties on fractal measure spaces, which is an important extension of the classical theory.

**Theorem 2.1.1** (Universal Property): For any associative algebra $A$ and linear map $\phi: V \to A$ satisfying $\phi(v)^2 = Q(v) \cdot 1_A$, there exists a unique algebra homomorphism $\tilde{\phi}: \mathrm{Cl}(V,Q) \to A$ such that $\tilde{\phi} \circ i = \phi$, where $i: V \to \mathrm{Cl}(V,Q)$ is the inclusion map.

**Rigorous Proof**:

1. **Existence**:
   - First, consider the tensor algebra $T(V) = \bigoplus_{n=0}^\infty V^{\otimes n}$, where $V^{\otimes 0} = \mathbb{F}$ (base field), $V^{\otimes 1} = V$, $V^{\otimes n} = V \otimes V \otimes \cdots \otimes V$ ($n$-fold tensor product).
   - Clifford algebra $\mathrm{Cl}(V,Q)$ is defined as the tensor algebra modulo the two-sided ideal generated by $\{v \otimes v - Q(v) \cdot 1 \mid v \in V\}$, i.e., $\mathrm{Cl}(V,Q) = T(V) / I$, where $I = \langle v \otimes v - Q(v) \cdot 1 \mid v \in V \rangle$.
   - There is a natural homomorphism $\phi_T: T(V) \to A$ on the tensor algebra, defined as:
     - $\phi_T(1) = 1_A$ (identity element)
     - $\phi_T(v_1 \otimes v_2 \otimes \cdots \otimes v_n) = \phi(v_1) \phi(v_2) \cdots \phi(v_n)$ (for any $v_1, \ldots, v_n \in V$)
   - Now verify $\phi_T(I) = \{0\}$:
     - For any $v \in V$, $\phi_T(v \otimes v - Q(v) \cdot 1) = \phi(v)^2 - Q(v) \cdot 1_A = 0$ (by assumption)
     - For any $a \in T(V)$, $b \in I$, $c \in T(V)$, $\phi_T(a b c) = \phi_T(a) \phi_T(b) \phi_T(c) = 0$, because $\phi_T(b) = 0$ ($b \in I$ is a linear combination of generators)
   - Therefore, $\phi_T$ induces an algebra homomorphism $\tilde{\phi}: \mathrm{Cl}(V,Q) = T(V)/I \to A$, satisfying $\tilde{\phi} \circ \pi = \phi_T$, where $\pi: T(V) \to T(V)/I$ is the quotient map.
   - Since $i(v) = \pi(v)$ for any $v \in V$, therefore $\tilde{\phi} \circ i(v) = \tilde{\phi}(\pi(v)) = \phi_T(v) = \phi(v)$, i.e., $\tilde{\phi} \circ i = \phi$.

2. **Uniqueness**:
   - Let $\tilde{\phi}_1$ and $\tilde{\phi}_2$ be two algebra homomorphisms $\mathrm{Cl}(V,Q) \to A$, satisfying $\tilde{\phi}_1 \circ i = \phi$ and $\tilde{\phi}_2 \circ i = \phi$.
   - Consider $\tilde{\phi}_1 - \tilde{\phi}_2: \mathrm{Cl}(V,Q) \to A$, which is a linear map, and for any $v \in V$, $(\tilde{\phi}_1 - \tilde{\phi}_2)(i(v)) = 0$.
   - Since $\mathrm{Cl}(V,Q)$ as an algebra is generated by $i(V)$ (because the tensor algebra is generated by $V$, and the quotient map preserves generators), therefore $\tilde{\phi}_1 - \tilde{\phi}_2$ is zero on the generators, and thus zero on the entire algebra.
   - Therefore, $\tilde{\phi}_1 = \tilde{\phi}_2$, uniqueness is proved.

**Physical Significance**: The universal property ensures the uniqueness of Clifford algebras in quantum field theory. Regardless of the specific representation (such as matrix representation, differential operator representation, etc.), their algebraic structure remains unchanged. This provides a solid mathematical foundation for the description of spinor fields in quantum gravity.
