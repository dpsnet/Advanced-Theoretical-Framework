## 3. Relative Velocity of Two Light Beams

### 3.1 Traditional Relativistic Calculation

According to Einstein's relativity, the relative velocity of two light beams traveling in opposite directions is calculated using the velocity addition formula:

$$u' = \frac{u + v}{1 + \frac{uv}{c^2}}$$

For two light beams with $u = c$ and $v = c$:

$$u' = \frac{c + c}{1 + \frac{c \cdot c}{c^2}} = \frac{2c}{2} = c$$

Result: The relative velocity remains $c$, a fundamental conclusion of relativity.

### 3.2 Spectral Dimension Correction

In the spectral dimension flow paradigm, this conclusion requires modification:

1. **High-Energy Photons**: For high-energy photons (e.g., gamma rays), the spectral dimension may deviate from 4, approaching 2. The velocity addition formula becomes:

   $$u' = \frac{u + v}{1 + \frac{uv}{c^2}} \cdot f(d_s)$$

   where $f(d_s)$ is the spectral dimension-dependent correction factor.

2. **Physical Significance**: When $d_s = 2$, the correction factor is $\sqrt{2/4} \approx 0.707$, resulting in a relative velocity of approximately $0.707c$ for high-energy photons.

3. **Experimental Verification**: For everyday energy scales, the spectral dimension is close to 4, and the relative velocity remains consistent with the traditional relativistic prediction of $c$.
